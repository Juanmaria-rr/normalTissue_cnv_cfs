1 · Scope

Question: whether interstitial copy-number breakends of normal breast epithelial cells (Lin et al. 2024, PRJNA1003661) overlap common fragile sites (CFS) more than expected under a positional null stratified by replication timing.

  • Data: 5-donor pilot (P07, P08, P18, P20, P36); 200 kb, 500 kb and 1 Mb bins analysed in parallel.
  • All tests are exploratory: there is no pre-registered primary hypothesis (decision of 4 November 2026). Three levels of multiple-testing correction are reported: q family (BH within one site set, over bins × directions × strata), q global (BH over every main-stratum row of every CFS set: bins × directions × sets) and q perm. global (the same on permutation p-values). The global ones are the relevant error control when no set was chosen in advance.
  • Revised on 5 November 2026 after an independent review: recalibrated depth rule, grid-aligned null with exact p-values, site-shuffle permutation p-values, cluster bootstrap by donor, test B with interval-based window assignment and chromosome-clustered errors, and robustness analyses (sections 6.6–6.8).
  • The cell-selection and breakend-calling criteria were fixed before any overlap with CFS was computed. The rules of the derived catalogues (consensus, breast-predicted, A+C, union) were written down before they were tested.

4 · Breakends and events

  1. States. On the QDNAseq segments (CBS, undo.SD = 1), segment ratio = segment value / median over the cell's usable bins of their segmented values; gain if > 1.2, loss if < 0.8 (permissive rule); consecutive segments with the same state are merged (length-weighted ratio) and merged segments with < 1 Mb of usable bins are dropped (a segment cannot reach 1 Mb by spanning a gap of filtered bins).
  2. Breakend. Boundary between consecutive retained segments of a chromosome with different states. Interval [lo, hi] = from the start of the last bin of the left segment to the end of the first bin of the right one, plus any gap of filtered bins.
  3. Deviation and stratum. Deviation = max |ratio − 1| of the two flanks. E1 0.2–0.3; E2 0.3–0.4; E3 0.4–0.6; E4 ≥ 0.6. The main definition (E2–E4) is the paper's 1.3 / 0.7 threshold.
  4. Direction. Loss or gain from the flank with the larger deviation; “mixed” when one flank is a gain and the other a loss.
  5. Type. Arm-level if [lo, hi] overlaps an approximate pericentromeric block ± 1 Mb (centromere and adjacent heterochromatin, UCSC acen/gvar/stalk bands; whole p arm of acrocentrics); interstitial otherwise. The tests use interstitial events only. “Interstitial” therefore means “not at the centromere”: the altered segment can still run to the telomere.
  6. Terminal. A breakend is terminal when its altered flank (the one with the larger deviation) is the first or last retained segment of the chromosome, i.e. the alteration runs to the chromosome end; otherwise internal (flanked on both sides). Used in the robustness split (6.7).
  7. Event. Within each donor, breakends of the same chromosome, direction and type linked within ≤ 2 Mb (single linkage on sorted positions). Event position, lo, hi and deviation = medians of its breakends.

6 · Test A: positional permutation

Null. Each interstitial event is re-placed on its own chromosome with its interval starting at a usable bin start (grid-aligned, as the observed intervals are), with the same width, without overlapping pericentromeric blocks ± 1 Mb, and only where the placement has the event's replication-timing quintile. The RT of a placement is computed exactly as for an event: mean RT of the usable bins its interval overlaps, quintile edges from all usable bins of that bin size. Candidate placements are equiprobable, so each event's null overlap probability pi is exact (all candidates are enumerated).

  • Observed O = events whose [lo, hi] overlaps the set; expected E = Σ pi; fold = O / E.
  • p exact: Poisson-binomial tail P(S ≥ O), S = sum of independent Bernoulli(pi), computed exactly. It assumes events are independent.
  • p perm. (main stratum): the set is shuffled 5,000 times (every site moved to a random usable bin of the same chromosome and RT quintile, width kept) and the fold is recomputed; p = (1 + #shuffled folds ≥ observed) / 5,001. It keeps the spatial clustering of events and of sites, so it is the reference p-value; it is usually larger than the exact one.
  • Fold 95% CI: 2,000 bootstrap resamples of events; donor CI: 2,000 resamples of donors (with 5 donors, a rough guide to between-donor variability).
  • q family / q global / q perm. global: see section 1.
  • Rows with expected < 2 events are greyed out: their fold is dominated by counting noise.

Revised 5 November 2026. The former null centred placements on bin midpoints and took the quintile of the centre bin only; events straddling an RT transition could get pi = 0 although they overlapped. p-values were Monte-Carlo (20,000 draws) and the only permutation control was 20 shuffles.

6.8 Cross-resolution: refining 500 kb breakends at 200 kb

The three bins are not independent replications when they use different cells (200 kb keeps only the deepest cells) and different interval widths. 7 · Test B: per-window regression

Windows: usable 1 Mb bins outside pericentromeric blocks ± 1 Mb (the same for the three event bin sizes). Rows: donor × window. Response: number of interstitial events assigned to the window; each event goes to the eligible window with the largest overlap with its [lo, hi] interval, and events overlapping no eligible window are excluded (counted in the table).

  • Covariates: overlap with the set (binary); mean RT, GC and mappability (z-scored, from the 50 kb annotation of sWGS_relativeCN); fraction of the window in genes > 300 kb; chromosome and donor as fixed effects; offset = log(fraction of usable 50 kb windows).
  • Models: crude (set + donor); adjusted (+ RT, GC, mappability, chromosome); adjusted_genes300 (+ genes > 300 kb). Large genes are probably part of the mechanism of fragility (a mediator, not only a confounder), so the last model is conservative: it removes part of the effect being tested.
  • Family: negative binomial (NB2) with alpha from the Cameron–Trivedi auxiliary regression on the Poisson fit; if alpha ≤ 0, Poisson. The standard error of the set is the larger of the model-based one and the one cluster-robust by chromosome (22 clusters): clustering keeps windows of a chromosome from counting as independent, but with few events and few clusters the sandwich estimate can be too small, so it is never allowed to shrink the error. In this run: poisson = 1053 of 1053 fitted models.
  • When no event (or every event) falls in windows of the set, the model has no finite estimate (separation); those 27 rows show “– (separation)” instead of an IRR of 0 with p = 0, and are left out of BH.
  • q family: BH within each set and model; q global: BH per model over the main-stratum rows of all CFS sets.

Revised 5 November 2026. Before, events were assigned by midpoint (about a quarter of the events were silently dropped, including 3 of the HS68 hits), separated rows reported p = 0, and errors were HC1 without clustering.

Unit: each interstitial breakend (E2–E4) of each cell, without collapsing clones, so an event weighs by the number of cells carrying it. For each breakend, o = overlaps the set; p = exact null probability (same chromosome, RT quintile and width, as in test A).

11 · Method limitations

  • 5-donor pilot; event counts range from tens to hundreds depending on the subset.
  • Resolution: breakend intervals span 0.4 to several Mb, the order of the site sizes.
  • Replication timing comes from the sWGS_relativeCN pipeline; its cell type is not documented.
  • Measured CFS catalogues come from other cell types (mostly tumour lines, lymphocytes and fibroblasts); the breast-predicted set is a prediction from accessibility and replication timing, not a fragility measurement.
  • Clonal collapse links within 2 Mb and requires the same direction and type; it can merge nearby independent events or split one event with different intervals.
  • Only breaks that leave a copy-number change are detected.
  • No primary hypothesis: only q global and q perm. global control the error across sets; q family does not. The strongest result (6.5) was selected from about 200 tests and needs confirmation in the full dataset with a test declared beforehand.
  • Events are clustered (recurrent regions, clones, donor-specific losses): the exact p and the event bootstrap assume independence and are optimistic; the permutation p and the donor bootstrap partly account for it.
  • Breakend intervals (≥ 2 bins) are often wider than the sites, so overlap measures proximity at the scale of the bin rather than a break inside the site; the refined 200 kb variant narrows this.
  • Test A matches chromosome and RT only; GC, mappability and gene size are handled in test B.
